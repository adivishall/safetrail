## Three bullets (use these)

> **Built a tourist geofencing engine on five hand-written spatial/temporal data
> structures in C++17 with zero external dependencies** — brute force, quadtree,
> R-tree, AVL interval tree, and a persistent path-copying quadtree — pruning
> 100,000 hazard zones to the ~99 actually near each query, for a measured **35×
> (quadtree) to ~240–260× (R-tree)** speedup over a brute-force baseline at
> 100,000 zones, **without ever changing the correct result**.

> **Made the speedups trustworthy by validating correctness first.** Every fast
> index is checked against a brute-force oracle over **18,000 randomized queries
> across three densities with 0 mismatches**, and two independent point-in-polygon
> implementations (ray casting vs winding number) agree on **100,000 points × 200
> polygons with 0 disagreements**. Benchmarks are the median of 7 passes; every
> figure is regenerated from one command and committed as CSV, so the docs can be
> diffed against the data.

> **Implemented the advanced structures, not just the easy ones.** A persistent
> (path-copying) quadtree that shares untouched subtrees by reference count —
> **13.0× structural sharing at 5,001 versions**, with past queries as cheap as
> present ones — for historical incident queries; an STR-bulk-loaded R-tree that
> is **33% smaller and 6.17× faster to query** than repeated insertion; and an AVL
> interval tree whose height stays at the balanced bound under churn. All backed by
> **39 test files / ~11,616 runtime checks, 0 failures**, clean under
> AddressSanitizer + UBSan, byte-identical across runs (`make determinism`).

## The measurements behind them

### Three spatial indexes, same data, same queries (`make bench` §1)

| zones | brute force | quadtree | R-tree (STR) | quadtree gain | R-tree gain | candidates/query |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 2.75 µs | 0.10 µs | 0.11 µs | 28.4× | 25.1× | 0.97 |
| 10,000 | 22.86 µs | 0.64 µs | 0.30 µs | 35.6× | 76.0× | 9.81 |
| **100,000** | **243.68 µs** | **6.95 µs** | **1.00 µs** | **35.0×** | **244.1×** | **98.78** |

The candidate count is identical across indexes — the speedup ceiling is output
size `k`, exactly `O(log n + k)`. The R-tree 100k figure is a **band (~240–260×)**,
the project's most scheduler-sensitive number; quote it as a band.

### Correctness — the reason the speedups mean anything (`make bench` §2)

| density | queries | quadtree mismatches | R-tree mismatches |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 / 500 / 5,000 zones | 18,000 total | **0** | **0** |

Ray casting vs winding number: **100,000 points × 200 polygons, 0 disagreements.**

### R-tree bulk loading — STR vs repeated insertion (`make bench` §3)

33% smaller tree, **6.17× faster query** at 100,000 zones; both builds `O(n log n)`.

### Persistent quadtree — path copying vs full copies (`make bench` §5)

| versions | sharing | query@past | query@now |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,001 | 11.3× | 0.35 µs | 0.40 µs |
| 5,001 | **13.0×** | 1.16 µs | 1.51 µs |

Sharing improves as history grows; a validity-only change allocates **zero** new
nodes (asserted in `tests/index/versioned_index_test.cpp`).

### Extensions (built on the core; `make bench` §6–14)

A* settles **77.7% fewer nodes** than Dijkstra (same optimal path); Hungarian
dispatch uses **15.7% less total travel** than greedy (never worse in 200/200);
adaptive sampling saves **99.1% battery** at 100% near-zone recall; k-d tree node
snapping is **48.6×** faster than a linear scan; a from-scratch SHA-256 Merkle log
(RFC 6962) for tamper-evident evidence.

## What NOT to claim

- **Not "faster than PostGIS/Boost".** Every speedup is vs *my own* brute force,
  the correctness oracle — not an external spatial library.
- **Not "tested on real tourists".** Real OpenStreetMap geography, *simulated*
  movement — simulation is what provides ground truth.
- **Not "~29,000× like the design doc guessed".** The measured, defensible number
  is ~35× (quadtree); the ceiling is output size `k`.
- **Not "production spatial engine".** Hand-writing the structures is a deliberate
  learning constraint, not a recommendation over mature libraries.
- **Not a constant R-tree speedup.** It's a ~240–260× band at 100k.

## Technologies actually used

C++17 (no external dependencies), CMake + Make, AddressSanitizer/UBSan, Python 3
(optional data-prep and plotting only), a self-contained HTML/Canvas dashboard
(no Leaflet, no tiles, no network).

Concepts: quadtree, R-tree (STR bulk loading), AVL interval tree, persistent
path-copying trees with structural sharing, ray casting / winding-number
point-in-polygon, state-transition detection, rollback union-find, Dijkstra/A*,
Hungarian assignment, Lamport clocks, Merkle logs, k-d trees, determinism
engineering.
